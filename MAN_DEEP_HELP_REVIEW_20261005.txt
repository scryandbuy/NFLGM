MAN DEEP HELP: CALLED SAFETY RESPONSIBILITIES
Base: dd9a9f2 (interception end-line fix, itself based on gameplay 0b4c49c).

Football motivation and restraint
Cover 1 needs its designated post safety; two-man needs both deep halves.
Assigning an extra slot receiver or tight end must use an available underneath
defender rather than silently consume that help. That can create an unfavorable
LB/receiver matchup: it is the real cost of retaining the called deep coverage.
Cover 0 still permits no help. Coaches retain their existing coverage choices;
this change enforces their selected responsibilities, not optimal play calls.
No generic extra tackler, receiver downgrade, or YAC/TD coefficient change.

Observed retained games
In each completed 2026 study (seeds 93031 and 93032), 50 passing touchdowns had
20+ YAC and exactly one selected pursuer. Of those, 27/21 were Cover 1/robber
with a dropped, unpaired safety absent from pursuit. This observation alone
does not prove the unpaired safety was the intended post.

Example: seed 93031, W1 DET-NO, drive 20/play 5, clock 588. Mauricio Blood
P0883 scores 54.7 yards, 21.5 air and 33.5 YAC. Kevin Gyllenborg P1784 is sole
pursuer. Public FS Phillip O'Rourke P1802 is recorded slot/man; Melvin Sample
P1844 is unpaired. Initial attributes make Blood beating Gyllenborg plausible.
The problem is the internally inconsistent safety assignment, not forbidding
that player or any particular touchdown.

Controlled reproduction
Explicit canonical NO defensive alignments reproduce the responsibility bug:
with three WRs, designated post P1802 supplies help. A fourth slot WR consumes
P1802 in assign_coverage, despite select_rush having protected him as the post.
The pursuit selector then excludes him because he is man-matched. Simply
selecting the other unpaired safety would mask the conflict by promoting the
low-hole/robber. The fixture uses named public defender attributes and labelled
control receivers; it is not a replay of a saved snap's discarded alignment.

Correction
- One shared helper identifies the man coverage's designated post/halves.
- Receiver assignment reserves these players and carries their identities in
  transient _unit.man_deep metadata.
- Exhausted preferred underneath pools use another unoccupied underneath man
  before assigning the same defender to two receivers. This fallback is scoped
  to calls that reserved deep help; pure-zone and Cover 0 behavior is retained.
- Pursuit honors the designated identities, actual live copies and occupied
  man responsibilities; no automatic promotion of a missing post.
- Named coverage supplies its canonical underneath principle for direct
  callers missing both under/man; explicit overrides still take precedence.
- No save schema or build artifacts changed. Main owns combined rebuilding.

Controlled effect, not a season forecast
5,000 paired direct YAC resolutions per variant, initial public Blood/NO
attributes, completed deep catch, initial contact 9.38 yards after catch:

Cover 1, 33.5 yards remaining: sole P1784 -> P1802 then P1784.
TD 2,721/5,000 -> 491/5,000; mean YAC 23.53 -> 13.81.
Cover 1, 60 yards remaining: TD 2,468 -> 409; mean YAC 37.21 -> 16.17.
Robber shares this fixture's result. Genuine breakaways remain possible.
Cover 0, Cover 2 and the unaffected two-man half are identical paired controls.
These deliberately conditioned catches are not league probabilities, full
game replays, or counterfactual point reductions. Completion/target decisions
may also change when actual underneath matchups change.

Verification
69 focused tests passed across test_man_deep_help, test_pass_pursuit,
test_coverage_recording, test_defensive_rush, test_coverage_development,
test_weekly_coverage_protection and test_interception_endline.
The new man-help file has 11 passing methods, including distinct five-man
assignments for 4WR+TE; target-TE, two-man sides/seams, zero-high, live/absent/
occupied helper, reordered inputs, named-call defaults and explicit overrides.
Independent review found and bounded the legacy-default and split-zone risks.

Unresolved, deliberately separate
The terminal HOUSE chase still ignores remaining distance and averages the
selected pursuers' chase ability. A controlled slow-TE/fast-DB case has the
same 62.52% conditional final escape chance with 20, 50 or 80 yards remaining.
That is a model weakness, not a justified replacement coefficient. A proper
follow-up needs individual recovery distance/angle/head-start evidence.
Opposite-half safety recovery on a long Cover 2 completion is also unmeasured;
do not automatically add another defender. Overall YAC/scoring calibration,
receiver opportunity distribution and injury frequency remain unresolved.
The frozen multi-season studies were not altered or restarted.
