FRANCHISE FOLLOW-UP — OCTOBER 5, 2026
Status: fixes integrated, independently reviewed and browser build verified

SCOPE AND EVIDENCE

Read-only sources: nflgm-2029-offseason-1.json (four retained seasons), and
nflgm-2030-offseason-10.json (the actual later SF continuation). Controlled
simulations use copies or fresh rosters. Historical outcomes span different
engine revisions and cannot isolate one causal change.

1. SCORING AND LEAGUE TALENT

The earlier paired 26.16-to-27.45 points/team comparison used identical fresh
rosters. Player improvement cannot explain that experimental difference.
In the actual save, points/team were 20.19, 23.34, 24.23 and 24.04. Passing YPA
fell from 7.14 to 7.00, rushing YPC from 4.54 to 4.41, while volume, passing TD
rate and sack rate changed. Broad offensive talent inflation is not established.

A current-code, 16-game comparison of fresh versus 2029 roster/coaching
contexts returned 26.22 versus 24.44 points/team, with wide uncertainty. It
changes both rosters and coaching and is not an isolated aging experiment.
Complete historic league attribute snapshots are absent; retained individual
snapshots, purchases, regression, career statistics and award history remain
usable evidence. See talent-trend-report.md and talent-context-games.json.

2. DESIGNED QB RUNS — IMPLEMENTED

The real healthy QB's mobility, security, condition, coach aggression, supporting
players, defensive pursuit/box and clock/score determine whether an already
called rushing play uses the QB. Normal blocking, fatigue, fumbles, injuries and
statistics apply. This does not replace the separate scramble decision.

The 32-game paired probe produced 87 designed QB runs for 425.7 yards, three
losses and one lost fumble. Running QBs received more opportunities, pocket QBs
showed restraint, and an injured mobile starter did not lend his attributes to
a replacement. Low realized counts for one mobile QB were traced to actual
opportunities and random outcomes rather than silently patched by name.
See qb-runs-report.md and qb-opportunity-summary.json.

3. SACK DISTRIBUTION AND HALF SACKS — CONFIRMED BUG FIXED

Unblocked rushers previously received the same 0.6-second arrival time; list
order decided ties. Actual path length, acceleration, speed and fatigue now
matter. A genuinely shorter rush path retains its advantage. Blocked contests
retain their prior calculations. The timing geometry remains a model assumption.

Two distinct finishers arriving within 0.12 seconds split credit 0.5/0.5. Team
sacks and QB sacks taken increase by one; escapes and nullified plays retain
no sack credit. Player history, narration and awards preserve the fractions.
The chosen shared-finish window needs continued observation, not an assertion
that every positional sack share is calibrated. See sacks-kicking-report.md
and half-sack-independent-review.md.

4. KICKING — SMALL COACHING BUG FIXED; NO GLOBAL ACCURACY CUT

The 51/51 under-50 sample included both teams over 20 GB games. The full 2029
league made 1,138/1,311 (86.8%). A favorable coach could reduce an elite short
kick from 99.5% to 99.0% because intermediate and final caps differed; those
caps now agree. Distance, ratings and weather remain active. No general
distance-curve or accuracy nerf was justified by the streak alone.

5. DEVELOPMENT, XP AND AGING

Four years of actual retained data were used. Checked XP purchases reconcile;
2026 missing dated-purchase history is not treated as zero development. Current
annual DEV logic allows a first credible poor season to lower DEV. A copied
2029 annual review produced 37 upgrades and 24 downgrades; it had not yet run
in the supplied offseason-step-1 save. Historical ratios reflect older builds.

Specialist ratings grew more than other groups, partly from replacing low-rated
long snappers and partly from kicker/punter accuracy development. The earlier
kicker XP reduction is retained. Saved FG accuracy did not rise consistently.
Longer controlled franchise talent distribution remains an open balance check.

6. AWARDS

Major offensive/defensive awards now require positive recorded production.
This fixes empty-evidence winners; it does not impose a positional quota.
The four historical OPOY backs had 2,179–2,404 scrimmage yards. The 2029 LB
DPOY remains first in a diagnostic removing tackle credit, so simply reducing
linebacker tackle weight is not a demonstrated solution. Rush opportunity,
forced-fumble production and award scoring remain separate watches. Historical
winners were not changed. See policy-report.txt and policy-diagnostics.json.

7. OWNER MOOD — IMPROVED

Current mood now compares W/L/T to seasonal expectations with owner patience
and a small-sample adjustment. Current champions are recognized. In the save,
CHI's 7–10 exceeds roughly 5.2 expected wins, while DET's 10–7 misses roughly
12.4. Their mood labels now reflect those different expectations. Read-only
display does not change RNG/history. Firing probability itself is not changed.

8. TRADES, ROSTER ALTERNATIVES AND SF

Confirmed fixes value only remaining first-season service, preserve later
contract control, and exclude a zero-service bookkeeping year from annual
acquisition cost, seller floors and public development credit. Independent
before/after rollover quotes now agree. Recent acquisitions retain their
normal evaluation period across calendar boundaries, without becoming immune
to meaningful upgrades or necessary roster cuts. Campbell–Darrisaw controls
retain both action and restraint without a player-name exception.

The actual newer SF save has zero unused picks in displayed drafts 2030–2032
and six each in 2033/2034. All 35 original picks for those five drafts reconcile
through logged ownership transfers. No pick-generation/disappearance defect
was found. Gibbs is a real immediate upgrade; the main problem is cumulative
spending. His transaction consumed two current firsts and the 2032 first.
Cash forecasts can improve when future rookies disappear, while losing their
cheap replacement opportunities was insufficiently reflected in willingness.

The new portfolio policy adds the cost of lost replacement options to CPU
willingness. It weighs remaining picks by quality and usable year, actual
expiring/aging/underfilled roles, public controlled successors and GM traits.
Retained displaced incumbents remain available; cash does not invent future FA
replacements. CPU trades, interactive offers/counters, pending inbox offers and
draft trades use the same assessment, with current-state checks before execution.

The corrected SF control adds 6.78 value units of opportunity cost to the Gibbs
package, versus 0.32 with three additional near-term classes. Extra capital only
in later drafts still leaves 6.37 of cost. A patient secure GM weighs it at
10.26; an urgent aggressive GM at 3.72. Gibbs still passes in this reconstruction:
the policy was not fitted to reverse a particular historical transaction.

An executed controlled sequence buys substantial WR and CB upgrades using real
owned picks, then rejects another substantial HB upgrade as stock is depleted.
The same last offer clears with intact draft stock. If the depleted club instead
has young players under five-year control, it can still spend its last pick.
These controls use disclosed synthetic offers and fixed comparable salaries to
isolate the shared guard; they are not a replay of SF's historical seller offers.

Review also corrected partial-package players being double-counted as full extra
successors, and prevented applying scouting error twice to an already observed
draft prospect. The new GM weights are policy assumptions, not empirically
calibrated probabilities. Several more franchise seasons are needed to assess
league-wide pick concentration and spending. See market-portfolio-followup.txt
and the independent portfolio review for limits and individual outcomes.

9. CONTRACT MODEL

Salary guarantees remain intentionally outside the model, as requested.

OTHER CONFIRMED FIXES

Departed coaches are not immediately rehired by the same club during the same
carousel; rivals and later reunions remain possible. Renewal reasons use the
actual decision. Reloading a historical championship bracket after rollover
does not create phantom new-year history or replay honors.
