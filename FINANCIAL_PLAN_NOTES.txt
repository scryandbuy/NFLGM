Scope
One deterministic four-cap-year preview now informs CPU free-agent offers,
renewals/options, franchise and RFA tenders, trade packages, waiver claims,
practice-squad moves, and routine restructure decisions. Each transaction keeps
the existing authoritative legal cap check. User choices are not subject to CPU
discretionary preferences. Emergency minimum-roster recovery remains available.

Funding
The projection includes actual remaining contract charges, dead money, unused
draft picks, and minimum funding for vacant roster spots. Signed rookies replace
their pick forecasts. Existing contracts are replaced, not stacked, on renewal.
IR pay is retained. Excess roster/draft slots receive a conservative displacement
credit: no more than a minimum salary, and no more than real net release savings.
This is a forecast, not a booked release or a guarantee of cutting named players.
Legal cap room never receives that forecast credit.

Joint AI bidding reserves exact candidate contracts within each bid batch.
Offers are reconsidered against the changed roster before resolution. The shared
projection is pure and rebuilt from saved roster/GM/contract state; it does not
persist a second ledger that can become stale. Existing accepted-offer/user
reservation infrastructure is preserved.

Routine restructures fund identified roster/draft shortfalls only. They no longer
raise every club to a fixed $12M target. Their future schedule may not worsen
negative future raw cap room. Existing emergency enforcement remains a separate
last resort. The new model does not add speculative future carryover.

Initial settings — NOT empirically fitted NFL coefficients
- Four-year horizon, with 5.5% conservative growth for unpublished future caps.
- Injury allowance: 0.8% of base cap, scaled by remaining season.
- Opportunity allowance: continuous GM patience/risk/aggression/security and
  contender inputs, bounded to 0.3–3.5% of base cap.
- Retention allowance: a 50% premium over replacement pay for selected leading
  players with expiring deals. Uses the greater of known pay and the median of
  up to three nearby veteran grades at the same position; identifiable original
  rookie deals are excluded. This anticipates raises from cheap rookie contracts
  without inventing an agent quote. Estimates refresh from current league state.
- Large roster improvement can release up to 80% of the soft cushion.
- Emergency required-role repairs can release all soft/completion reserves;
  existing hard legal checks still apply.

Deliberately not implemented
Ownership cash limits or CBA cash-floor enforcement. Cap charge, APY and cash
paid are different measurements; old saves do not supply complete historical
cash payments. Staff owner_spend and the game's PS salary cap exemption are
unchanged. No historical cash shortfalls are invented.

Validation and calibration limits
Behavior tests cover exact schedules, pending bids, future roster funding,
GM differences, exceptions, atomic refusals, user control and save/load.
audit_financial_plan.py compares identical initial seed states against an
immutable baseline through free agency, draft and final roster compliance.
It advances contracts but skips games, injuries, retirement and regression;
draft order is synthetic alphabetical. Different acquisitions change later
random draws. This is an accelerated behavior probe, not a complete season or
proof of long-term franchise balance.

Validation goal clarified by user
Prioritize believable GM trade-offs over fitting precise NFL reserve percentages.
Historical deadline panels and injury-replacement examples supply context; more
data collection is not a prerequisite. Test needs, future obligations, urgency,
flexibility and emergency repairs. Multiple seeds and multi-season runs remain
necessary before claiming stable long-term talent or competitive balance.

Behavioral follow-up — 2 October 2026
The user clarified that believable GM thinking is the goal; precise historical
reserve fitting is not required. Further data collection was stopped.

Confirmed and corrected: an expiring rookie star could produce zero retention
allowance because his old pay was below replacement salary. Nearby same-position
veteran contracts now inform the placeholder. It remains a soft planning cost,
refreshes without randomness or persisted quote caches, excludes identifiable
original rookie contracts, and disappears once an actual renewal covers that year.
The new regression was demonstrated to fail under the old current-pay-only rule.
