Football reasoning and alternatives
Explosive repeated work can justify a breather before deep fatigue. A useful
reserve, a rest-conscious coach or a long series makes that more attractive;
a weak reserve, brief series or important passing down can justify another
starter snap. No hard snap-share quota or player-name exception is used.
Healthy QB/OL continuity keeps its existing policy.

Physical recovery was previously coarse and duplicated at drive boundaries.
Correcting it alone gave many edge starters nearly all healthy snaps because
their between-series recovery exceeded their work, and substitutions waited
until severe fatigue. The final combination records each physical work/rest
event once and uses earlier EDGE/DT/HB breather decisions based on real reserve
quality, current condition, coach preference and the existing down context.
Stamina still changes work; no extra random substitution follows this choice.
Coach preference refreshes at actual selection and restore, including staff
changes. Recovery event counters are saved/restored. False starts do not create
work/rest opportunities; nullified live snaps, kicks and tries are accounted.

Healthy HB rotation now compares actual healthy backs. WRs needed elsewhere
in the formation no longer distort the starter/backup quality gap; emergency
cross-position replacements remain possible when no healthy HB is available.
If every eligible back needs rest, choose the freshest rather than reverting
to an exhausted starter.

QB safe endings and consequences
A QB with space can give up yards to slide or step out. Continuing through
contact remains an option, influenced by running strength, awareness, fatigue,
coach preference, first-down value and clock situation. A safe ending never
adds yards to obtain a conversion. Designed runs stop before their modeled
first contact; after-contact yards cannot be retained while deleting the hit.
Existing touchdowns and sneaks are excluded from voluntary-ending decisions.

The final outcome reaches clock runoff, timeout spending, tackle credit,
injury exposure, fumble occurrence, narration and the book. A safe ending
removes the carrier-hit premium but retains ordinary non-contact injury risk,
running workload and the unforced fumble component. If a loose ball occurs,
it supersedes the proposed slide/boundary finish; no post-dead-ball fumble is
narrated. No invented tackler or forced fumble is credited on a safe ending.
Late OOB stops until the snap, ordinary OOB restarts on spotting; effective
period metadata covers regular/playoff OT. It follows regulation quarter
transitions within a drive. The five-second ordinary spotting allowance is
an approximation, not an NFL-measured constant.

An additional actual-carrier gap emerged in game review: a called run from an
empty backfield used the QB without the optional qb_run flag, so it missed
the running exertion increment. Actual QB run outcomes now receive that work
exactly once and are labeled as QB runs (sneaks retain their separate label).
This also covers actual QB carries on tries without inventing another snap.
