Confirmed findings

1. Final cap cleanup destroys completed CPU rosters.
Seed 93030, 2027 DEN: cutdown 53 active / -$20.295m current cap; final wire
releases 17 and leaves 36 / +$0.021m. LS remains uncovered. Actual Week 1
kickoff is DEN 36, BAL 53, both IR 0. Week 2 DEN has 39. Weekly refill reaches
53 by Week 9 but cap space becomes -$19.772m.

Seed 93031, 2027:
DEN: cutdown 53/-$36.161m -> final wire 23/-$0.791m, 30 releases.
NO:  cutdown 53/-$17.525m -> final wire 38/+$0.171m, 15 releases.
KC:  cutdown 53/-$3.873m -> final wire 50/+$0.042m, four releases plus addition.
DEN has two unfilled WR assignments, CB, P and LS at the final gate. Multiple
normal offensive/defensive package assignments contain fewer than 11 players.

Relevant existing flow: cutdown.finalize fills through emergency_fill after
its limited cleanup passes; Session.step_clear_wire then runs CT.enforce with
target=0 and does not refill. contracts.enforce calculates its roster reserve
once; _fix_one's additional cuts increase replacement costs without updating
that target. These are mechanism observations, not a validated fix proposal.

2. CPU trades can create 54-player rosters which reach kickoff.
Seed 93030 Week 1: LV buys WILL P0389 Ja'Sir Bentley (79.13 OVR, age28.2,
$1.195m) from CAR for LV2026R7. LV53->54; CAR53->52. Actual Week 2 LAC/LV
kickoff has LAC53/LV54 with no IR for either team. An independent short probe
reproduces this: outputs/cpu-trade-roster-limit-probe.json.
Trades execute and sync cap without a corresponding roster-limit repair;
the game caller has no 53-player gate. Those execution lines predate the
recent package-aware decision changes. Not evidence that all recent changes
caused this problem.

3. Weekly practice-squad refill can bypass the cap beyond minimum survival.
practice_squad.keep_groups_whole calls call_up(emergency=True) for ordinary
fill-to-53, role repair and group shortage. Emergency skips require_room.
Seed 93031 CIN reaches -$0.644m at Week 9 and -$0.552m at Week 18. Its logged
examples include IR of HB P0510 followed by QB P2577 promotion, and IR of CB
P0492 followed by SS P0546. Seed 93030 DEN's subsequent refill compounds #1.
This broad emergency policy is existing behavior; a fix must explicitly
decide how to fund complete rosters rather than silently ignore the cap.

4. Temporary draft/camp over-cap states are allowed; final resolution matters.
Both first offseasons finish FA with all teams currently cap compliant.
After the draft, 28/32 are over the current offseason cap in each seed.
After UDFA camp, 31/32 are over in each seed. Minimum-body reserves do not
cover rookie slot premiums. Draft._select signs rookie contracts without a
cap gate; later cleanup is relied upon. User explicitly permits this order:
see the rookie class, then cut/restructure to get compliant. Do not enforce
a hard draft-time affordability rule or classify intermediate over-cap as
a defect. #1 demonstrates failure of resolution in2027. The2028 and2029
primary offseasons BOTH recover to53/cap-compliant rosters for all32 teams.
WAS still lacks LS at2028 gate; all2029 starting roles are filled. Forecast
feasibility and commitments without requiring all rookie costs prepaid.

5. Same-offseason commitment reversals recur; dead money alone is not a defect.

Seed93030: six new deals cut before Week1; $15.817m current dead charges.
           One extension cut; $2.115m current/$6.345m following-year dead.

Examples: NO CB P0327 Cam Reddish signed $9.16m/1y, then cut for $4.334m dead.
NO MIKE P1826 Kamari Kloska extended $4.47m APY/4y, then cut for that split.

do not equate all of it with a newly paid extension bonus without tracing.
Minimum/camp/draft churn is reported separately in summary.json.
User policy: a costly cut can be sensible after decline, loss of a starting
job, or inability to trade the player. Do not classify cuts solely by dead
charges. Follow-up finds explainable cuts in these examples:
- Kloska stays78.773 from extension to camp, then is displaced by FA Mitch
  Orange82.274 and rookie Joel Marcelin79.83. No decline; a lost-role case.
- Reddish stays83.126 and the CB group is unchanged between his signing and
  camp. NO moves from +6.981 after FA to -24.408 after draft/camp, making cap
  relief necessary. His release alone is not evidence of a bad decision.
- MIA Thompkins stays84.328 but same-round FA Chris Works87.65 takes the lead
  SS role. His release moves cap -10.405->-0.555 by saving9.850. A defensible
  lost-role/cap cut despite substantial dead charges.
The concern to investigate is earlier repeated commitments and insufficient
future reserve. Failed shopping attempts are not logged by this observer;
no claim is made that these players could have been traded, or that an
available acceptable offer was refused.
Further user clarification: newly signing a player, buying another at his
position and immediately cutting the first should be rare. A defensible
last-step cut does not excuse avoidable acquisition churn. MIA signs both
SS Thompkins ($16.34m APY/3y) and SS Works ($29.10m APY/3y) in FA round1,
then cuts Thompkins before a game. Ratings have not declined. Their immediate
roster gains were +15.94 and +17.06 respectively; cutting Thompkins later
loses12.74. Thus both can contribute in package allocation, but the budget
and retention plan cannot sustain the combined commitments.
market.ai_bids prices candidates against one pre-round roster;
resolve_phase does recheck current RN.move_gain>1, but keeps the original
price/term. It does not require a coherent funded role plan for both recent
acquisitions. This is not an entirely stale-roster bug, nor an argument for
banning two acquisitions at any shared position (WR/CB/TE have multiple jobs).

Limits and interpretation
This is a small-seed, short longitudinal audit, not proof of 30–40-year safety.
The baseline is frozen; subsequent fixes in other chats are not included.
In particular, the user's later PS-pay cap exemption implemented by the main
chat is NOT in this baseline. It may improve squad size/funding; active
contract affordability, roster-limit enforcement and acquisition planning
must be verified separately on that updated code.
Main independently replayed fill_squads on the two saved2027 checkpoints with
PS-pay exemption 21b781e: seed93030 short squads10->0 (91 added); seed93031
13->0 (152 added), with cap unchanged by those signings. This is a reported
follow-up on newer code, not part of the frozen longitudinal results.
Same-seed pre-change comparison was not run for the entire calendar. Source
history identifies older execution paths, but emergent frequency can change
when new roster preferences interact with them.
Current cap_space respects phase/top51; full_cap_space charges all contracts.
Use current cap for offseason legality; reports retain both explicitly.
Season_closed rosters >53 include healed IR players and are expected during
the offseason. Only kickoffs prove actual oversized game rosters. Under53
alone may reflect normal short-term injury vacancies; 23–39 and missing
roles after cleanup are material failures.
No duplicate ownership or wrong-team membership was seen in completed first
cycles. Final package failures arise from roster shortages, not repeated IDs.
Street alternative samples are limited to six highest-OVR same-position
players, and do not establish best affordable legal alternatives.
